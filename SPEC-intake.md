# Spec: intake

- **Module id:** `intake` (see [CAPABILITIES.md](CAPABILITIES.md))
- **Depends on:** `platform`
- **Decisions:** [ADR 0001](docs/decisions/0001-mvp-decisions.md) entries 1, 4 and 7, the input defaults, and amendments A7, A8 and A11
- **Conventions:** [SPEC-platform.md](SPEC-platform.md) Part A; job states from platform B9
- **Status:** approved by the owner on 2026-10-06, after two review cycles

## Objective

Get a job post from the owner's phone into storage as one job, as fast as the network allows, start the clock for both arms of the timing comparison, and recover when a phone upload breaks. Intake accepts screenshots and pasted text, never fetches a link, never shrinks text below legibility, and never lets image bytes pass through a Vercel function.

## Inputs

1. **Upload (always available).** The New job page offers "Choose screenshots" (`<input type="file" accept="image/*" multiple>`), a "Paste text" box and Start.
2. **Share target (extra).** The manifest declares a share target: `action` `/share-target`, method `POST`, `multipart/form-data`, with `title`, `text`, `url` and `files` named `images` accepting `image/*`. The service worker intercepts that POST, because routing it to a Vercel function would hit the 4.5 MB body limit. It then:
   - refuses a share with no images and no text, with "Nothing arrived from the share. Use Choose screenshots instead.";
   - refuses a share holding only a link, with "LinkedIn's share sends only a link, which jobkit never opens. Take screenshots of the post and share those." A share holds only a link when it has no images and a link in its `title`, `text` or `url` field, with fewer than eight other words in its `text` (fewer than 25 around a LinkedIn link, on `linkedin.com` or `lnkd.in`, whose share of a job names the company and the title) and no email address there: LinkedIn's share sends a link alone or with a few words around it, while a post naming an email address is one jobkit can apply to, however short. Links are found as in item 3; the worker keeps its own copy of those rules, which its tests hold to `links.ts`'s answers;
   - refuses a share larger than jobkit takes (more than 8 images, one over 40 MB as shared, before encoding, or more than 20,000 characters of `title` and `text` as they are joined), with "This share is more than jobkit takes: at most 8 screenshots of 40 MB each and 20,000 characters of text. Share less, or use Choose screenshots."; a `url` field over 2,000 characters is not kept. Known: the browser has read the whole body by the time the worker checks its sizes; each opening the worker counts rewrites the share's record; a web page's `intent:` link to the installed app's share activity is taken as blocked by Chrome, untested;
   - refuses every share while the server's share-target switch is off (below), with "Sharing to jobkit is off for now. Use Choose screenshots instead.";
   - otherwise stores the files and text (the share's `title`, when the text does not already hold it, then its `text`) in IndexedDB under a share ID, in place of any share no page took, and redirects to `/jobs/new?share=<id>`, which takes them out of IndexedDB, drops the share ID from the address (so a reload or Back takes nothing again), puts them in the form, and starts the upload with no further tap; a start that fails leaves the screenshots, text and link in the form, and Start sends them as a share. A browser that cannot store the share is told "This phone could not keep the shared screenshots. Use Choose screenshots instead." The job's clock starts at the share's arrival, measured by the wall clock, which the worker and the page share.
   - Another site's page can post to the share target too, so a share starts untapped only when the page's request says, in its `Sec-Fetch-Site` header, that the owner caused it (`none`: the share sheet, the address bar), and only on the share's first opening, since the reload of a page that failed to load looks like the owner's own doing: the worker counts each time New job opens with the share's ID, and the page counts a reload or Back, which may skip the worker, as no first opening. No jobkit page posts a share itself, so `same-origin` counts only in the dev server the end-to-end tests start, which post one from jobkit's own page. An upload kept from before (step 3 of the upload path) is finished first, and then the share waits for the tap too, as it does while another tab finishes one, since it may be the same post shared again. A held share's form says why, another site first: "This share may have come from another site. Check it, then press Start.", "Another tab is finishing an upload from before, so this share waits for your tap. If it is the same post, clear it.", "An upload from before came first, so this share waits for your tap. If it is the same post, clear it.", "This share was opened before, so it waits for your tap. Check it, then press Start." or, after an untapped start that failed, "This share's start did not finish. Check it, then press Start." Its clock still starts at its arrival, kept under the server's day.
   - A held share goes back into IndexedDB, marked with why it waits, and shows again whenever New job opens, so leaving the page or reloading it loses nothing; Start takes it out first, so it starts one job whichever tab taps ("This share was started in another tab. Look for it in the Jobs list."), and "Clear the share" removes it.
   - A held share shows as Start will send it: each screenshot as the upload prepares it (its first frame, drawn on white) at the form's full width, its text and its link (as text, never as a link) without the characters item 3 drops. Start opens for it only once the page has had no tap for two seconds, so a page that lures the owner into tapping again and again cannot land a tap on Start. Choosing other screenshots waits until the share is cleared. When the share is gone, the page says so, and an upload kept from before is still finished, without opening its job. An untapped start whose upload is kept for New job to finish leaves nothing in the form to start again.
   - Once this month's spend reaches the cap for new jobs, the page drops a share and starts nothing. A share no page took within a day is forgotten.
3. **Pasted text.** Up to 20,000 characters, from the box or from a share's `text` field. Characters that show nothing and only hide text are dropped from it and from the share's `url` field: NUL, the tag characters, and the overrides, embeddings and isolates that turn text around on screen, so the owner sees what the model reads; the direction marks and the joiners, which written Urdu and emoji use, stay. A URL in the text or in the share's `url` field is stored as a reference and never fetched: each web address as written (the share's first), without the sentence marks around it, at most 10, each at most 2,000 characters, in the job's `post_links`.
4. **Adding screenshots to a job.** The job page offers "Add screenshots", with a purpose: `post` (more of the post) or `form` (an application form's screening questions). Adding `post` images moves the job to `extracting` for a fresh `extract`. Adding `form` images runs `extract_questions`, then drafting and judging for the new questions only.

**Share-target switch.** Turning the share target off in the manifest takes effect only when Chrome next updates the installed app, which can take days. So the switch lives on the server (`SHARE_TARGET_ON` in `src/server/shareTarget.ts`, served by `GET /api/share-target` with no session, since it says nothing about the owner): the service worker reads it (cached for at most an hour; when the server cannot be asked within 5 seconds, or answers with an error, it keeps the share) and refuses shares while it is off. The day-1 spike tests the share target on the owner's phone, because a third-party report says Chrome 153 on Android delivers share requests with no parts at all; if it fails, the switch goes off and the upload button carries the flow.

## Upload path

1. The client prepares each image:
   - decodes it at native resolution, its first frame, drawn on white as a screenshot is, so text on transparent pixels reads as it will to the model. Tall scrolling screenshots stay tall; extraction cuts them into tiles. Only an image wider than 2,576 pixels, which phones do not produce, is scaled down to that width;
   - refuses an image taller than 30,000 pixels with "Too tall. Take two shorter screenshots instead.";
   - encodes it as WebP at quality 0.9 when both sides are at most 16,383 pixels (WebP's limit), otherwise as JPEG at 0.92;
   - computes its SHA-256 and drops exact duplicates within the job;
   - computes how many tiles it needs, with the extraction spec's formula.
2. `POST /api/jobs` sends only metadata: count, sizes, dimensions, hashes, tile counts, the input kind (`page` for the New job page, `share` for the share target), the share's `url` field, the pasted text, and `clientElapsedMs`, the monotonic time between the share's arrival (or the Start tap) and this request. The server checks every limit again, creates the job and assigns its timing arm (ledger spec). A manual-arm job starts in `manual_run`, uploads nothing, and the page shows the manual-run screen. Otherwise the job starts in `uploading` with its image rows, and the server returns one signed upload URL per image, each for a single path `jobs/<jobId>/<imageId>.<ext>` in the private bucket. Supabase fixes such URLs' validity at two hours.
3. The client uploads each file straight to Supabase Storage, with up to three attempts per file. If the page reloads or the network drops, `POST /api/jobs/<id>/upload-urls` issues fresh URLs for the files not yet stored, and the client resumes. The browser keeps the upload in IndexedDB until the server has it, and New job finishes it when it opens again:
   - one tab at a time, under a Web Lock per job where the browser has Web Locks (secure contexts only: HTTPS, or localhost), reading the kept upload again under the lock, since another tab may have forgotten it;
   - asking for fresh URLs only before the page's deadline, three times at most while the connection, the server or a proxy fails (a dropped connection, 408, 429 or 5xx), since each request for them logs an attempt; past it, uploading nothing more and asking for the confirmation alone, which says how the job stands and queues it when every file arrived, as when a confirmation's answer was lost. The page asks for that confirmation too before it calls any upload too slow, whether the deadline passed during an upload or while it waited to ask again. The deadline counts by the page's own clock and by the wall clock, which goes on while a phone sleeps;
   - forgetting the kept upload once nothing more can come of it or the page says to press Start again, while every message that keeps it says jobkit goes on if New job opens again within 15 minutes of the start (a signed-out owner or a device no longer approved is told so too, and an answer that says nothing of what arrived keeps it to check again), and leaves nothing in the form to start a second time; and forgetting the kept uploads, without asking, once even the newest is a minute past the server's 15 minutes, all but any kept since. A browser that could not keep the upload says so instead, and to check the Jobs list before pressing Start again. Starting another job leaves a kept upload alone. Known: when the browser fails to forget a kept upload, a later visit may finish it.
4. `POST /api/jobs/<id>/uploaded` asks the server to confirm that every object exists with the declared size. The job then moves to `queued` and the runner starts `extract`. A confirmation that arrives within the 15 minutes is judged on what had arrived, even when checking the files takes it past them, unless the timeout moved the job meanwhile.
5. A job still `uploading` 15 minutes after it began uploading (at creation, or at Upload again) moves to `upload_failed`; the job page offers "Upload again" and Skip. Upload again opens New job for that job, which takes screenshots alone and no share, with nothing of a New job form carried over: the owner chooses every screenshot again, the phone prepares them as for a new job, and, holding the job's upload lock from before its request, `POST /api/jobs/<id>/upload-again` checks every limit and the spend cap again, replaces all the job's screenshots with them in one transaction, moves the job back to `uploading`, which starts its 15 minutes again, and starts an upload attempt; whatever arrived of the failed upload is removed once the answer has gone. The upload and its confirmation then go on as for a new job, a reload of the page finishing what the browser kept, and the job keeps its pasted text, links and start. Pressed while an upload of the job is under way, as after an answer that was lost or an upload that stalled, it restarts it in the same transaction: the upload under way fails, as its timeout would fail it, with its attempt, and the new one begins its own 15 minutes; a job not waiting for its screenshots is refused. A page that read the job before its upload was begun again elsewhere learns that its own upload failed. Objects that arrive after that are deleted by the purge.

Route handlers in this module reject request bodies over 64 KB, so image bytes cannot reach a function by mistake. Every upload attempt and its outcome is logged, so the Stats page can report upload success (the one-pager's 95% target): a job's first attempt starts as it is created, with its files' count and bytes; each request for fresh URLs ends the attempt before it as failed and starts another with the files left; a confirmed upload ends its attempt as ok. The upload timeout, the only other way out of `uploading`, ends the open attempt as failed. Known: a request for fresh URLs whose answer was lost, asked again, logs one attempt that never ran.

## Limits

- At most 8 images per job, each at most 10 MB after encoding, and at most 20 tiles in total, the most one model request can carry without lower image limits. A job over the tile limit is refused with "Too many screenshots for one job. Remove some, or split the post into two jobs."
- Pasted text at most 20,000 characters.
- The New job page refuses to start when the ledger reports the monthly cap reached, and says so; it still finishes an upload begun before.

## Clock

The job stores `received_at` (server time of `POST /api/jobs`) and `client_elapsed_ms`. Its start time is `received_at` minus `client_elapsed_ms`: the moment the share arrived on the phone (for a share held for the tap, too), or the moment the owner tapped Start. This is the start event for both arms of the timing comparison, because the arm is assigned to the job at creation (ledger spec).

## Storage and retention

- One private bucket, `jobkit-private`, with no public access. The server reads with the Supabase secret key; the browser sees images and crops only through signed URLs valid for 60 seconds.
- `job_images` rows hold the job ID, order, purpose, storage path, width, height, bytes, SHA-256, tile count, `created_at`, `purge_after` (30 days later) and `kept_for_eval`.
- The daily purge (platform B6) deletes, from storage and from the tables:
  - images and crops past `purge_after`, unless kept for evaluation;
  - objects under a job's path that no image or crop row names, once the job has left `uploading`: one that arrived after an upload timed out, or that an upload begun again left behind;
  - pasted raw text older than 30 days, with the links found in it.
- `npm run eval:pull` copies kept images to the laptop's evaluation folder and then clears `kept_for_eval`, so the 30-day rule deletes the cloud copy (amendment A7).

## Acceptance criteria

1. Uploading three screenshots creates one job with three images in order, stored under the job's path. No route handler receives image bytes; a body over 64 KB is refused.
2. Encoding: a 1,080 × 6,000 screenshot keeps its resolution; a 3,000-pixel-wide image is scaled to 2,576 pixels wide; a 1,080 × 20,000 image is encoded as JPEG; a 1,080 × 31,000 image is refused with the message above. Every image's SHA-256 and tile count are recorded; duplicates within a job are dropped.
3. Tile limit: two 1,080 × 30,000 screenshots (26 tiles) are refused by the client and again by the server.
4. Share target, end to end with a synthetic POST: images start an upload with no further tap; a share another site's page sent, and one opened again after its page failed to load, wait for the tap, showing the screenshots and the link, and "Clear the share" empties the form; an empty share, a link-only share (one with a few words around the link, or a link in the title alone, too), one larger than jobkit takes, and any share while the switch is off are refused with their messages and create no job, while a short share naming an email address starts one; a new share takes the place of one no page took, and one no page took within a day is forgotten; at the spend cap, a share starts no job and is not kept.
5. A paste-only job works. A LinkedIn URL in the text is stored and never fetched; the test fails on any outgoing request to it.
6. A ninth image and text over 20,000 characters are refused with clear messages.
7. Upload recovery: after a simulated reload mid-upload, fresh URLs are issued for the missing files only, and the job completes. A job still `uploading` after 15 minutes moves to `upload_failed`; a late object is deleted by the purge.
8. Confirming a job whose objects are missing or the wrong size leaves it `uploading` with the missing files listed; creating or confirming a job needs an approved session.
9. With a fake clock, the purge deletes expired images and crops that are not kept, keeps kept ones, clears old pasted text, and `eval:pull` clears `kept_for_eval` after copying.
10. The job's start time equals `received_at` minus `client_elapsed_ms`.
11. Adding `post` screenshots moves a `kit_ready` job to `extracting`; adding `form` screenshots runs `extract_questions`, then drafting and judging for the new questions.
12. The Stats page's upload success rate counts every logged attempt.
